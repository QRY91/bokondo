About

Glenn Bokondo · software engineer · Lanaken, Belgium

I build integration-heavy systems and run them in production. For the past year I have been putting large language models into real workflows, with the checks that keep them honest. I work in Dutch, French and English.

I started on the phone

Four years at Carglass's customer contact centre, inbound and outbound, in Dutch and French. It taught me what a procedure looks like to someone who is busy and sceptical, and it is why I write user guides before anyone asks for them.

A bootcamp, then thrown in deep

A C# bootcamp at Train4SmartServices (2019–2020), and months later I was at Oakfields working on enterprise logistics for Maersk: API integrations with customs systems, client calls, releases. Scope that usually comes with a team. I learned fast because I had to.

Then I ran a product by accident

The product was Mileviewer, a logistics monitoring platform for Maersk's European offices. When the lead developer left there was no hand-over plan, so I wrote one: documented the import pipeline nobody understood, cleaned the data, built the pipelines and the monitoring, shipped the releases, supported the users, went to Amsterdam to watch people use the thing. The product survived its people. Everything I have built since is designed to do the same.

Then I went back to school

A graduaat in Programming at PXL (2023–2025), with an internship at BornDigital, a digital product studio, where I built Panopticron, a project-health dashboard, as my capstone. Earlier, unfinished studies in psychology and game design still shape how I do user research and interface work.

A year of consultancy

At bttmline (2025–2026) I built for SME clients: an ERP quotation platform taken live in one month, a customer-support assistant that drafts replies in three languages, automation against government portals. The numbers are on the home page.

What people bring me in for

  • Integrations nobody else wants: ERP, SOAP, OAuth2, government portals, legacy imports. Usually the part of the project that has already failed once.
  • Taking a half-built system to production. Tests, monitoring, a runbook, and a go-live date that holds.
  • AI assistants and automation that stay honest. Retrieval over your own data, validators, human review, evaluations you can rerun.
  • Data pipelines and search over large catalogues. Millions of rows, bounded memory, measured latency.
  • Being the one engineer when the lead leaves. Analysis, release management, support, and the hand-over document.

I can work from the first version

Most projects I have joined did not come with clean specs. I turn a fuzzy brief into written acceptance criteria, build the smallest thing that proves the integration works, and then harden it. The quotation platform went from first meeting to live in one month that way.

The stack is not the point

TypeScript and Node most days, Python and Go when they fit, Rust and C for the things that must not fail. PostgreSQL, Docker, Kubernetes, Ansible. I run my own three-node homelab with local model inference, which is where the agent research happens.

AI, with restraint

I build LLM tools that people use daily, and I build what keeps them honest: evaluations you can rerun, actions gated and logged, a human in the loop where it matters.

Proof points

  • Three and a half years of production work, every system of it integration-heavy.
  • One ERP platform designed, built and live in one month: 415 of 421 commits.
  • One SaaS run solo for a Fortune Global 500 client for a year.
  • Fifty-plus public repositories; some thirty-five essays on qry.zone.
  • Dutch native, French C1–C2, English C2.
